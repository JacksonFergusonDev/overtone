# Changelog

What changed in each release of Overtone, for people using it. Add entries under Unreleased as user-visible changes land.

## Unreleased

- Initial repository scaffold for the Overtone desktop app and CLI.
- The desktop app and `overtone-cli` share one library database in `~/Library/Application Support/com.jacksonferguson.overtone/`. Pass `--library <path>` (or set `OVERTONE_LIBRARY`) to use a different one.
- `overtone-cli status` shows track, missing track, vibe, and needs-review counts. With `--json`, errors are also printed as JSON.
- `overtone-cli scan [folders…]` indexes your audio files in place, reading them without ever modifying them. Rescans only look at new or changed files, tracks whose files disappear are kept and marked missing, and folders on unplugged drives are left alone. Duplicate detection compares audio content only, so the same recording with different tags is still recognized. `scan --refresh` rereads every file's tags, and scans do this automatically after an Overtone update improves tag reading.
- Scans read title, artist, album, BPM (with decimals), key, genre, comment, grouping, and stream details such as sample rate, bit depth, and whether an `.m4a` is ALAC or AAC.
- `overtone-cli audit` checks audio quality and flags likely fake lossless files (MP3s wrapped as FLAC or WAV), upscaled MP3s (a 128 kbps file re-encoded at 320), and fake hi-res files, with the measured cutoff and a plain-language reason. Nothing is ever deleted or moved. Review flagged tracks with `ls --needs-review`, render a spectrogram with `spectrogram <track>`, and record your verdict with `review <track> ok|replace|clear`. Your reviews survive reanalysis.
- `overtone-cli tag mirror` writes each track's vibes as hashtags into its Comment tag for Serato smart crates, Rekordbox intelligent playlists, and Traktor smartlists, keeping all other Comment text. It previews by default; `--write` applies. Every write is verified against the original before it replaces the file (Serato and Traktor cue data, artwork, and audio must be byte-identical, and the ID3 version is kept), and files Overtone can't edit safely are skipped with a reason. M4A files aren't written yet. `tag restore --write` puts back the original Comments.
- Comment mirroring now edits only the Comment itself and copies every other byte of the file unchanged, so it works on files it used to skip (tags with sort-order frames, duplicate frames, unusual dates or languages, and FLAC files from some taggers). On a test library of 1,082 files it now writes 1,067, up from 1,024.
- Overtone now records where each metadata value came from: the file, you, or a lookup you accepted. `overtone-cli meta show <track>` shows the source of every field, `meta set` and `meta reset` manage your own values (which rescans never replace), and `meta suggestions`, `meta accept`, and `meta reject` review lookup suggestions, which never change a track until you accept them and never touch Comment, Grouping, or your Genre. See [Your metadata and where it came from](docs/metadata.md).
- `overtone-cli audit` now analyzes files its own decoder can't read (HE-AAC, Opus, WMA, and damaged files) with ffmpeg when it's installed, and HE-AAC files from Apple's encoder are no longer misread as low-quality. The first audit after updating reanalyzes every track once.
- `overtone-cli audit --integrity` decodes every file in full with ffmpeg and records decode errors, such as corrupt frames or truncation. `ls --damaged` lists tracks with errors inside the music, and `show <track>` lists the errors. Junk at the very start or end of a file, common in MP3s and harmless, is reported separately. Files are only read.
- `overtone-cli meta lookup` searches MusicBrainz, and AcoustID and Discogs once you add a key with `meta config`, for each track's album, release date, label, catalog number, ISRC, track number, and store genre. Everything it finds waits as a suggestion for you to accept or reject; nothing about your tracks or files changes until you do. AcoustID identifies tracks from their sound, so it works even when tags are wrong.
- Audits now measure each track's integrated loudness (LUFS), loudness range, true peak, clipping, and silence before the music starts; `overtone-cli show <track>` lists them. Nothing about the audio is changed.
- `overtone-cli export` writes copies of tracks or a whole vibe into a folder as CDJ-safe AIFF (16-bit, 44.1 kHz), AAC, MP3, FLAC, or WAV, with your tags and artwork. Originals are only read and existing files are never replaced. See [Export copies](docs/export.md).
- `overtone-cli convert` turns WAV and AIFF tracks into FLAC beside the originals, checked sample for sample, with tags, artwork, and Serato cues, loops, and beatgrids carried over. It previews by default and lists anything that can't move to FLAC; `--write` converts. Originals are untouched and stay the tracks Overtone and your DJ software use until relocation lands. See [Convert to FLAC](docs/convert.md).
- New `overtone-cli` commands: `roots add|ls|rm` to choose folders to index, `ls` (with `--vibe`, `--missing`, and `--needs-review` filters), `show <id or path>`, and `vibe ls|create|rename|delete|add|rm` for overlapping, nested vibes.
- `overtone-cli sync serato` writes each vibe as a Serato crate under a top-level `Overtone` crate, leaving crates you made yourself untouched. Add `--dry-run` to preview the changes. Overtone refuses to sync while Serato DJ is running and backs up every crate file it replaces.
- `overtone-cli sync rekordbox` writes your vibes as nested playlists in a `rekordbox.xml` file under an `Overtone` folder (in Overtone's data folder by default, or `--out <path>`). Point Rekordbox at it in Preferences > Advanced > Database > rekordbox xml, then import the playlists. The export carries no cue or beatgrid data, and `--dry-run` previews it.
- `overtone-cli sync traktor` writes your vibes as playlists in an `Overtone` folder in Traktor Pro 3 or 4's `collection.nml`, changing nothing else in the file. Tracks that aren't in your Traktor collection are listed and left out. Close Traktor first: Overtone refuses to sync while it runs and backs up the collection before every write. Pass `--collection <path>` if you have more than one Traktor version installed, and `--dry-run` to preview.
- `overtone-cli sync rekordbox-tags` writes each vibe as a My Tag (named by its full path, such as `Dubs/Deep`) in an `Overtone` category of Rekordbox's own database, so you can filter by vibe in Rekordbox without importing anything. Your own My Tags are untouched, and tracks Rekordbox doesn't have yet are listed and left out. Quit Rekordbox first: Overtone backs up the database, edits and checks a copy, and only then replaces it. `--dry-run` previews the changes.
- The desktop app now runs your whole library: a fast, sortable, filterable track list with quality badges and vibe pills, playback with a clickable waveform, a quick-tag palette (`T`) for adding vibes to several tracks at once, and an inspector (`I`) showing each track's spectrogram with Verify OK and Replace wanted actions. Needs review walks you through flagged tracks one by one. See [Using the desktop app](docs/desktop-app.md) for every shortcut.
- The desktop app's Sync view previews and applies Serato crates, Rekordbox XML exports, Rekordbox My Tags, Traktor playlists, and Comment tag mirroring, shows whether Serato, Rekordbox, or Traktor is running, and lists past syncs. Settings adds library folders and runs scans and quality audits with live progress; an audit can be cancelled and keeps the tracks it finished.
- The desktop app's inspector now shows each track's loudness and decode errors, where every metadata value came from, and lookup suggestions to accept or reject, and lets you edit fields, export a copy, or convert a WAV or AIFF track to FLAC. Settings adds lookup keys, **Look up new tracks**, **Check files**, and batch FLAC conversion, each with progress and Cancel. See [Using the desktop app](docs/desktop-app.md#check-metadata-sound-and-files).
- Overtone can now write the values you set or accepted from lookups (title, artist, album, album artist, remixer, label, genre, year, BPM, key, and grouping) into your MP3, WAV, AIFF, and FLAC files, so your DJ software sees them. Use `overtone-cli meta apply --write`, **Write to file…** in the inspector, or **Write metadata…** in Settings. Each write is checked so Serato cues, artwork, other tags, and the audio stay exactly as they were, and `meta restore --write` or **Restore the file's original values** puts the old values back. See [Write your values into your files](docs/metadata.md#write-your-values-into-your-files).
